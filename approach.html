How I actually work
through a site.

Every urban intervention, network audit, or community vision begins with the same principle: don't propose anything until you understand what is already there and why. Whether evaluating a single high-conflict suburban intersection, synthesizing hundreds of local survey responses, or tracing a century of built-form history, this methodology bridges ground-level empirical evidence with high-level policy frameworks to deliver clear, actionable planning roadmaps.

Step 01, Context N ↑
METHOD 01 / 04

Start with what's already on the ground

Before sketching options or proposing interventions, I establish a robust empirical baseline across physical, historical, and demographic layers. Ground-truth data reveals how a site functions in reality, surfacing historical constraints and spatial patterns that zoning maps alone often obscure.

Key Data Sources & Tools
  • Census profile breakdown & demographic benchmarking
  • Historical aerial time-series & fire insurance atlases
  • First-hand site photography & streetscape spatial audits
Step 02, Diagnostics N ↑
METHOD 02 / 04

Name the actual conflict

Rather than relying on qualitative impressions like "this street feels chaotic," I isolate specific infrastructural breakdown points. Separating underlying causes from superficial symptoms turns subjective urban complaints into precise spatial diagnostics.

Key Data Sources & Tools
  • Kevin Lynch imageability mapping (paths, edges, nodes, districts, landmarks)
  • Integrated SWOT analysis across mobility, architecture, & land constraints
  • Quantitative safety exposure metrics (crossing widths, speeds, refuge availability)
Step 03, Policy N ↑
METHOD 03 / 04

Check it against what the city already promised

I benchmark conditions directly against statutory plans, Vision Zero frameworks, and official active transportation strategies. Comparing adopted municipal promises against site-level conditions transforms design proposals into an accountability check, backed by local resident survey feedback.

Key Data Sources & Tools
  • Policy alignment scorecards against Official Plans & Vision Zero
  • Resident survey synthesis (trip purpose, mode preference, comfort levels)
  • Precedent analysis & regulatory gap reviews
Step 04, Phasing N ↑
METHOD 04 / 04

Propose something that can actually get built

I avoid ungrounded masterplans in favor of phased, capital-aware roadmaps. Recommendations are divided into immediate operational fixes, mid-term capital construction, and long-term land-use retrofits so that implementation can begin right away.

Key Data Sources & Tools
  • Short, medium, and long-term phased intervention roadmaps
  • Before & after concept massing & street cross-sections
  • Trade-off matrices & constraint mapping

Frameworks I keep coming back to

Analytical lenses used across studies to evaluate built environments consistently.

FRAMEWORK 01

Kevin Lynch Imageability

Evaluating urban legibility through paths, edges, districts, nodes, and landmarks. This framework measures how intuitive a place feels on foot or by transit, providing clarity beyond standard land-use zones.

FRAMEWORK 02

Policy Alignment Scorecards

Directly mapping on-the-ground metrics against Official Plans, Vision Zero pledges, and active transit targets to highlight policy gaps with complete clarity.

FRAMEWORK 03

Community Survey Synthesis

Structuring lived community experiences, satisfaction levels, and travel habits into prioritized data points so resident needs directly shape corridor strategies.

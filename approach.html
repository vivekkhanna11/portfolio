Approach

How I actually work through a site.

I use roughly the same process whether I'm looking at one intersection or a whole neighbourhood's history. It's not a formula so much as a habit: don't propose anything until you understand what's already there and why.

Step 01, Context N ↑
METHOD 01 / 04

Start with what's already on the ground

Before I sketch anything, I want the base layer right: boundaries, land use, demographics, housing type, and as much historical aerial imagery as I can find. You can't fix a plan you haven't actually looked at.

Key Data Sources & Tools
  • Census and neighbourhood profile data
  • Old fire insurance atlases and historical aerial photo series
  • Site photography and resident survey data
Step 02, Diagnostics N ↑
METHOD 02 / 04

Name the actual conflict

It's easy to say a place "feels unsafe" or "needs more density." I'd rather measure it. Once the problem has a shape through Kevin Lynch mapping, SWOTs, or crossing metrics, the solution stops being a guess.

Key Data Sources & Tools
  • Lynch framework: paths, edges, nodes, districts, landmarks
  • SWOT analysis (Mobility, Architecture, Constraints)
  • Safety exposure & speed threshold metrics
Step 03, Policy N ↑
METHOD 03 / 04

Check it against what the city already promised

Holding a site up against Official Plans, Vision Zero targets, and cycling plans turns a design opinion into an accountability check. Gap analysis reveals where policy commitments fall short on the ground.

Key Data Sources & Tools
  • Policy alignment scorecards
  • Community survey synthesis and comfort indexes
  • Precedent and regulatory framework reviews
Step 04, Phasing N ↑
METHOD 04 / 04

Propose something that can actually get built

Avoiding all-or-nothing masterplans by breaking proposals into clear implementation tiers: quick wins with paint, medium-term construction, and long-horizon grid rebuilds.

Key Data Sources & Tools
  • Phased implementation roadmaps
  • Before & after massing concepts
  • Constraint & trade-off mapping

Frameworks I keep coming back to

A few lenses that show up across most of the plates in this portfolio.

FRAMEWORK 01

Kevin Lynch Imageability

Paths, edges, districts, nodes, and landmarks. I use this to understand how legible a place is on foot, which often has nothing to do with its formal zoning designation.

FRAMEWORK 02

Policy Alignment Scorecards

Comparing what's actually built against Official Plans, Vision Zero commitments, and cycling network plans, so the gap between promise and reality is stated plainly, not implied.

FRAMEWORK 03

Community Survey Synthesis

Letting resident-submitted data, comfort levels, trip purpose, and satisfaction actually drive the priorities, instead of guessing who uses a corridor and how.

How I actually work
through a site.

I use roughly the same process whether I'm looking at one intersection or a whole neighbourhood's history. It's not a formula so much as a habit: don't propose anything until you understand what's already there and why.

Step 01, Context N ↑
METHOD 01 / 04

Start with what's on the ground

I start every project by gathering physical and historical context. Combining census profiles, site photography, and historical maps gives me a clear picture of how a space functions in daily life before I draft any recommendations.

Key Data & Tools
  • Demographic benchmarking & census breakdowns
  • Historical aerials & fire insurance atlases
  • First-hand site photography & spatial audits
Step 02, Diagnostics N ↑
METHOD 02 / 04

Isolate the root conflicts

Instead of making broad claims about a site, I pinpoint specific physical breakdown points. Whether measuring unsafe crossing widths or mapping legibility barriers, I turn subjective urban complaints into clear, actionable data.

Key Data & Tools
  • Kevin Lynch imageability mapping
  • SWOT analysis across mobility and land constraints
  • Quantitative safety & exposure metrics
Step 03, Policy N ↑
METHOD 03 / 04

Align with municipal policy

I check site conditions directly against Official Plans, Vision Zero targets, and active transportation goals. Factoring in local resident survey feedback ensures recommendations align with both city commitments and community needs.

Key Data Sources & Tools
  • Policy alignment scorecards against Official Plans & Vision Zero
  • Resident survey synthesis (trip purpose, mode preference, comfort levels)
  • Precedent analysis & regulatory gap reviews
Step 04, Phasing N ↑
METHOD 04 / 04

Build realistic, phased solutions

I avoid unrealistic plans in favor of clear, capital-aware roadmaps. I break down fixes into immediate, medium, and long-term phases so cities and planners can act on them right away.

Key Data & Tools
  • Short, medium, and long-term implementation roadmaps
  • Before & after street cross-sections
  • Trade-off matrices & constraint mapping

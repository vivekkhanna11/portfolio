Urban planning, from the street level up.

I'm an urban planning and transportation design student, and most of my work sits somewhere between community planning history, active transportation strategy, and contextual urban design. I like moving between scales. Some studies are about a single arterial crossing, others trace a century of change across an entire neighbourhood, but I try to keep both grounded in mapped evidence and actual municipal policy instead of design gestures that just look good on a slide.

Recent studies include a policy alignment audit of a suburban Scarborough retrofit node, a historical and cross-cultural read on Forest Hill South's planning values, a Kevin Lynch based imageability analysis of the Seneca Newnham campus, and a community survey driven cycling network strategy for downtown Perth, Ontario.

I'm honestly more interested in the details people skip over. Turning radii. Crossing exposure time. The gap between a city's stated Vision Zero commitment and what's actually built at an intersection. Finding that gap and turning it into something buildable, in stages, is what I enjoy most about this work.
